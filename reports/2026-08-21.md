## P0: DeepSource's price is wrong by 4x, in DeepSource's favour

`/vs/deepsource` currently says, twice:

> "Free for OSS; Business from ~$6/user/month"
> "DeepSource's Business plan starts at approximately $6/user/month, which is cheaper
> than Optibot's $29/user/month."

DeepSource's own pricing page has no Business plan. The only publicly priced paid tier is
**Team at $24 per user per month billed yearly**.

So the page invents a plan name and understates a competitor's price by roughly 4x, then
uses that invented number to concede a price disadvantage that does not exist. Against
the real $24, the gap to $29 is five dollars, not twenty-three.

This is the most expensive kind of error on a comparison page, because it costs you
twice: a prospect who checks sees an inaccuracy, and a prospect who does not check walks
away believing a competitor is a quarter of your price.

**Source:** https://deepsource.com/pricing, fetched 2026-08-21
**Caught by:** `competitor-deepsource-price`, a crosscheck rule added on 2026-08-17

### Two claims about your own product with no published source

Both currently live on /vs/greptile and /vs/coderabbit:

- **"+43% more edge cases caught per PR review"**
- **"2x more security vulnerabilities found"**

No benchmark, methodology or third-party study is cited anywhere on the site or in the
research corpus behind this file. These are not wrong, they are **unsourced**, which is a
different and more awkward problem: a technical evaluator who asks "compared to what,
measured how" has no page to be sent to.

There is a live example of why this matters. Greptile's founder published his v5 release
on 2026-08-05 using behavioural metrics instead of a benchmark: "great catch" replies per
PR (0.266 to 0.342), and a strict address rate, the percentage of comments applied
exactly as recommended (52% to 66%). That framing is defensible in a way a bare
multiplier is not, and it is closer to the mergeability argument Optimal's own CTO makes
publicly.

**Recommended:** either publish the methodology, or restate both as "in our evals".

## Checker defect found this run

`unlimited-gone-comparison` reported "NOW PRESENT on 1 of 10: /vs/coderabbit". It is a
**false positive**. The string it matched is:

> "Does CodeRabbit have unlimited reviews? Not in practice."

That is a question about a competitor, correctly answered, not a promise about Optibot. A
plain substring test cannot tell the two apart. The rule needs a proximity condition
before it can be trusted, and until then it must not be acted on. Recorded in
`FACTS.tsv` so next week's run does not re-raise it as news.

This cuts both ways: this is the first finding this system
has produced that would have caused a wrong edit if followed. The rule that saved it was
requiring a quotable line and a link for every verdict.
