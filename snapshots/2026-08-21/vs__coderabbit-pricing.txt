 Three separate meters 
 Three ways usage-based pricing gets expensive

This isn't about CodeRabbit being a bad reviewer. It is about a pricing structure with three separate meters running at once. All three figures below are from CodeRabbit's published pricing.

 5 reviews / dev / hour 
 Per-developer rate limits
 Pro allows 5 reviews per developer per hour (10 on Pro Plus, 12 on Enterprise). A separate fair-usage policy tightens that further for heavy users, stepping Pro down to 1 sequential review per hour once a developer passes 60 reviews in 7 days.

 $0.25 per reviewed file 
 Usage credit add-on
 Credits are $1.00 each and cover $0.25 per reviewed file. They apply to reviews that exceed a plan limit, so a 200-file refactor that Pro would otherwise skip costs about $50 to review on demand.

 $0.50 / agent minute 
 Per-minute agent billing
 CodeRabbit's fixing agent is billed by runtime. There is no flat ceiling, so a heavy month of agent runs has no predictable upper bound.

 Optibot vs CodeRabbit Pricing — Common Questions

 Is CodeRabbit or Optibot cheaper? It depends on how much your team ships. CodeRabbit starts lower per seat ($24/user/mo on Pro, annual), but the seat is only part of the bill: security is a $40/user/mo add-on, the fixing agent is $0.50 per minute, and credits at $0.25 per reviewed file are what let a throttled developer keep reviewing without waiting. Optibot is flat: Plus is $29/user/mo and Pro is $49/user/mo (annual), with the security agent and engineering metrics in the Pro seat and no usage surcharges. Compare the bundle you actually need rather than the headline seat price.

 Why does usage-based pricing get expensive for fast-moving teams? CodeRabbit meters usage three ways. First, a per-developer review rate limit of 5 reviews per hour on Pro (10 on Pro Plus, 12 on Enterprise), which a fair-usage policy tightens further once a developer passes the 95th percentile of recent usage, stepping Pro down to 1 sequential review per hour at 60 or more reviews in 7 days. Second, a usage-credit add-on at $1.00 per credit covering $0.25 per reviewed file, which is how a throttled developer keeps reviewing without waiting for the window to reset. Third, a fixing agent billed at $0.50 per minute. The throttle does not raise your invoice on its own, but it does mean throughput falls exactly when your team is shipping hardest unless you buy credits.

 Does Optibot have a per-PR or per-file charge? No. Optibot is flat-rate per user. A review costs the same whether it touches one file or one hundred. Each plan includes a monthly deep-review allowance (50 on Plus, 100 on Pro, up to 350 on Max). If you reach the monthly allowance, deep reviews pause until the next billing cycle or you can upgrade your plan to restore them immediately.

 How accurate are the cost figures on this page? All hard pricing claims about CodeRabbit (seat prices, the 5/10/12 per-hour rate limits, the fair-usage ladder, the usage-credit add-on, and the $0.50/agent-minute rate) come from CodeRabbit's published pricing and documentation. The $0.25 per reviewed file rate and the $1.00 per credit price are also published, in CodeRabbit's usage-based add-on documentation. Note that the rate limits are hourly rather than monthly, and that credits are optional: a throttled developer can wait for the rolling window to reset instead of buying them. Verify current pricing on CodeRabbit's site before making a decision.
